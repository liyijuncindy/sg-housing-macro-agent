**Possible sales-market channel:** A rise in the SORA benchmark may lift floating-rate mortgage servicing costs and reduce borrowing capacity as loan resets flow through, which could restrain demand for home purchases; an easing in the benchmark could work in the opposite direction.

**Possible rental-market channel:** Higher ownership financing costs may shift some households toward renting while also raising landlords' holding costs, so the net effect on rents could be in either direction.

**Possible lag:** Pass-through may unfold over subsequent months depending on mortgage pricing, reset timing, fixed-rate periods and refinancing terms, so housing responses could lag movements in the benchmark.

**Limitations:** The series is a month-end snapshot rather than a monthly average and is a benchmark rate, not an individual mortgage offer; mortgage spreads, fixed-rate loans, refinancing terms and policy constrain pass-through, so the benchmark alone cannot determine prices.
